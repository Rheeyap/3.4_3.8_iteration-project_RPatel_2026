SKILLS:


A specialised field hockey shooting technique where players use a dragging, whipping motion to generate exceptional speed, power, and controlled aerial lift, mainly during penalty corners to beat goalkeepers and defenders.

How to do one: To perform a drag flick, approach the ball low and balanced, cradle it on the stick while dragging forward, rotate hips and shoulders explosively, then snap wrists upward during release, transferring body weight forward for maximum power, height, accuracy, and control.


The tomahawk is a powerful, advanced striking technique in field hockey (sometimes called a reverse-stick hit or backhand sweep). Rather than using the flat, open side of the stick, a player rotates their hands into a reverse grip (like holding a frying pan) and swings the stick horizontally from right to left to strike the ball with the stick's edge.

How to do one: To perform it, you position the ball slightly to your side, grip the stick normally, bend your knees, and rotate your shoulders and hips to generate power. As you swing, you keep the stick low, sweep through the ball smoothly, and use a slight wrist snap on contact, following through toward your target. It’s important to stay balanced, use body rotation rather than just your arms, and ensure you always hit with the flat side of the stick, not the rounded back, to keep the shot legal and controlled.


A flick is used to lift or propel the ball with a quick wrist action instead of a full swing. It is often used for passes and shots, especially during penalty corners.

How to do it: Stay low with the ball in front, slide the stick under it, and snap your wrists to lift and direct the ball. Use good timing and control, and avoid swinging the stick too much.
