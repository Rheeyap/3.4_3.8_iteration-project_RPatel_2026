Skills

A drag flick is a specialised shooting technique where the player drags the ball with a sweeping, whip-like action to generate speed, power and lift. It is used almost exclusively on penalty corners, where the extra time on the ball lets you beat the keeper with placement rather than pure strength.

How to do it

Approach the ball low and balanced, with your weight over your knees. Cradle the ball on the face of the stick and drag it forward in one smooth motion, rotating your hips and shoulders as you go. Snap your wrist upward through the ball at the last moment to add lift, then transfer your body weight forward to finish with accuracy and control.

The reverse stick hit (sometimes called a tomahawk) is played off the opposite side of your body, using the back face of the stick. It lets you pass, shoot or clear the ball without turning to reposition, which is essential once you're moving at pace or the ball is on your left.

How to do it

Rotate your top hand so the stick face turns outward, keeping your wrists relaxed and your elbow high. Get your body low and side-on to the ball, then push through it with a short, controlled swing rather than a big wind-up. Follow through low to keep the ball on the ground and accurate.

A flick lifts the ball off the ground with touch rather than power, ideal for chipping over a defender's stick or lofting a pass into space. It relies on wrist and stick angle more than swing speed, so it stays controlled under pressure.

How to do it

Set the ball slightly ahead of your front foot with the stick face open. Keep your bottom hand loose and let it slide down the shaft as you push through the ball, opening the face further at the point of contact to lift it. Finish with your wrists rolling upward, not your arms swinging wide.
